## What Missed Calls Actually Cost You

Before spending $85 or $149 a month, do the math on your own business.

Say you run a two-tech plumbing company and miss four calls a week while you're on jobs. If even one of those four would have turned into a $350 repair, that's about $1,400 a month you never see. An AI receptionist at $85 to $149 a month only has to catch one of those calls to pay for itself.

The numbers are smaller for a solo house cleaner (see our guide on [how to start a cleaning business](/how-to-start-a-cleaning-business/) for typical rates), where a new customer might be worth $150 a visit. But recurring customers stick around for months, so one caught call can still be worth a lot more than the monthly fee.

If you mostly miss calls after hours, even a basic message-taking plan helps. If you miss calls during the day because you're busy, you want one that books jobs straight onto your calendar.

## AI Receptionist vs Answering Service vs Missed Call Text Back

These three get mixed up. Here's how they differ:

| | What happens when you miss a call | Typical cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Missed call text back** | The caller gets an automatic text ("Sorry we missed you, how can we help?") | Often included in phone or CRM tools |
| **AI receptionist** | An AI voice answers, asks questions, takes details, sometimes books | $29 to $400/mo |
| **Human answering service** | A live person answers and takes a message | Usually more per call than AI |

Missed call text back is the cheapest fix and better than nothing, but plenty of homeowners with a burst pipe won't wait for a text conversation. They'll call the next plumber on Google. An AI receptionist answers the phone, which is what they wanted in the first place.

A traditional human answering service for small business still makes sense if your customers are older or the calls are complicated, like quoting a full renovation. Smith.ai is one of the few that offers both AI and human options.
